    How to use this lab

    Quick start

    1. Fill in the x-axis and y-axis cards. Axis title is the quantity in words (e.g. Speed); Symbol is the short letter used in equations (e.g. v). The graph is labelled “Speed v / m s−1”. Then add the unit and uncertainty.
    2. Type your measurements into the table. If you repeated the measurement, set Trials first.
    3. Choose a Fit above the graph. The equation and R² appear on the graph and under Analysis.
    4. Export the graph and the tables for your report.

    Tick See example at the top of the Data panel to see a finished pendulum experiment. Untick it to get your own graph back, exactly as you left it. Clear all under the table empties the title, axis cards and data.

    Entering data

    • Type values as you recorded them. 1.00 and 1 are different measurements, and the program keeps exactly what you typed.
    • Enter or ↓ moves down a column. A new row appears whenever you fill the last one.
    • You can paste whole columns from a spreadsheet: click the first cell, then paste.
    • Uncertainty can be absolute (± 0.01) or a percentage (± 2 %). Tick Per-point uncertainties if each row has its own value.
    • With several trials (up to 12), each point is the mean. Choose how the error bar is worked out: half the range (the larger of half the range and the instrument uncertainty, usual in physics and chemistry), standard deviation (how spread out the trials are, usual in biology), or standard error (SD / √n, how precisely the mean is known). The graph says which one it shows.
    • The ○ button at the end of a row excludes that point from the fit; × deletes the row.

    Strict precision

    When this is on, every value must be written to the same decimal place as its uncertainty. With ± 0.01 you must write 1.00, not 1 or 1.003. Problem cells turn red and the graph stays blank until they are fixed. Hover over a red cell to see what to write.

    Tables

    Raw data shows what you typed, with headings in the form Quantity symbol / unit ± uncertainty. Processed data shows means, uncertainties and any linearised values, each rounded so the value matches its uncertainty. Use Copy to paste a table into Word or Google Docs, or save it as PNG or CSV.

    Fits

    Lineary = mx + c
    Proportionaly = kx (through the origin)
    Quadraticy = ax² + bx + c
    Powery = axⁿ
    Exponentialy = Aekx
    Logarithmicy = a ln x + b

    All fits use least squares on the points you have not excluded. Numbers are rounded to match the significant figures of your data (change this under Axes & display).

    Straightening a curve

    Use Plot x as and Plot y as to graph, for example, T² against L, or ln V against t. Error bars are recalculated for the new quantities. A straight line on the new graph tells you which relationship the data follow, and lets you use max/min lines.

    Max/min lines

    With a linear fit, tick Max/min lines. The program finds the steepest and the shallowest straight lines that pass through every error bar. It then reports the gradient and intercept with their uncertainties: Δm = (mmax − mmin) / 2. If no single line passes through all the error bars, it says so and uses the corners of the first and last error bars instead. Treat that as a sign to look again at your uncertainties or for outliers.

    Tools on the graph

    • Exclude points: click a point to leave it out of the fit (it turns hollow). Click it again to bring it back.
    • Tangent: click anywhere on the graph, or type an x value under Analysis, to draw the tangent to the fitted curve and read its gradient.
    • Residuals: shows how far each point is from the fit. A random scatter about zero means the model suits the data; a pattern means it does not.
    • If the error bars are too small to see at the plotted scale, a note says so under the graph.

    Analysis panel

    Readouts give the intercepts and let you read y for a chosen x, or x for a chosen y. Compare with a literature value gives the percentage difference. With max/min lines, it also says whether the accepted value lies within your uncertainty range. Statistics compares the repeated trials in each row: a one-way ANOVA across all rows, or a t-test between two rows, with the p-value and the critical value at the 5 % level. Copy results as text copies everything for your report.

    Saving your work

    • Your work is kept in this browser automatically, so it is still there when you come back.
    • Save file downloads your graph as a file that you can reopen later with Open…, on any computer.
    • Share link makes a link that contains all your data. Send it to your teacher or a lab partner. Nothing is stored on a server.
    • Download the graph as PNG for a report, or SVG for the sharpest print. Copy image lets you paste it straight into a document.

    Opening one of those links replaces whatever is on screen here. If you are in the middle of another graph, press Save file first.

    Conventions

    • Table headings take the form Quantity symbol / unit ± uncertainty, e.g. Length L / m ± 0.002.
    • Uncertainties are quoted to 1 significant figure (2 if you choose), and each value is rounded to the same decimal place as its uncertainty.
    • Right and up are positive, so left and down are negative. g = −9.81 m s⁻².
    • Gradient uncertainty: Δm = (mmax − mmin) / 2.

    Devices

    Use a computer, or a tablet in landscape. Phones are not supported. The graph grows to fill the screen, so a bigger window gives a bigger graph. Your work is stored only in this browser on this device: use Save file or Share link to keep it or move it.
